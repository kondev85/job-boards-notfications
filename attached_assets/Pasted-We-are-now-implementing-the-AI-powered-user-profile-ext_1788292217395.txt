We are now implementing the **AI-powered user profile extraction layer**.

The purpose of this feature is to make the application completely **user-agnostic**.

Do NOT design the profile around Project Management, Product Management, Technology, Gambling, or any other specific profession or industry.

The system must be able to analyze ANY professional CV and discover the person's actual experience, expertise, industries, strengths, gaps, and transferable capabilities.

For example, one user might be a Project Manager, another might be a DevOps Engineer, another might have Construction Management experience, another might be a Financial Analyst, and another might be a Product Manager.

Gemini must discover this from the CV rather than being given a predefined list of expected skills.

---

## 1. Database changes

We already have a `users` table and a `profile_text` column.

Add a new column:

`profile_json JSONB`

Do not create another table for this.

The two fields have different purposes:

### profile_text

A concise, readable professional profile generated from the CV.

It should summarize the person's background, experience, expertise, industries, strengths and relevant characteristics in natural language.

### profile_json

The structured representation of the person's professional profile.

This will later be used by the matching engine.

---

## 2. Gemini's job

Gemini should read the user's CV and extract the person's professional profile WITHOUT assuming any particular profession.

The analysis should discover:

### Professional identity

Identify the main professional areas represented in the CV.

Examples could include:

* Project Management
* Product Management
* Software Engineering
* DevOps
* Cybersecurity
* Finance
* Accounting
* Sales
* Marketing
* Construction Management
* Architecture
* Legal
* Operations
* Data Analysis
* HR
* Procurement
* Customer Success
* Compliance
* Engineering
* or any other area actually supported by the CV.

Do not restrict the output to these examples.

### Experience areas

Identify the distinct areas in which the person has meaningful professional experience.

For each area, determine:

* area name
* strength of experience
* approximate years of experience where reasonably supported
* evidence from the CV
* recency
* seniority/responsibility
* whether the experience appears core, secondary, or incidental

Do not invent years or experience.

### Skills and capabilities

Extract the person's meaningful professional skills.

Group them where useful into categories such as:

* technical
* managerial
* operational
* analytical
* commercial
* communication
* leadership
* domain-specific
* tools/software
* methodologies

Do not use a fixed predefined skills list. Discover the skills from the CV.

### Industry/domain experience

Identify the industries and business domains in which the person has worked.

Examples might include:

* gambling / gaming
* fintech
* cryptocurrency / blockchain
* banking
* insurance
* software
* SaaS
* telecommunications
* construction
* real estate
* healthcare
* manufacturing
* retail
* logistics
* consulting
* government
* etc.

Again, these are examples only.

For each significant industry/domain, assess:

* strength of experience
* approximate duration where reasonably clear
* recency
* depth of exposure
* evidence

Distinguish between someone who spent years working in an industry and someone who merely worked on one project involving that industry.

### Technologies, tools and methodologies

Extract technologies, software, platforms, tools, frameworks, certifications and methodologies that are genuinely supported by the CV.

Examples could include:

* Python
* AWS
* Kubernetes
* Jira
* Salesforce
* SQL
* SAP
* Agile
* Scrum
* PMP
* etc.

Do not assume technical proficiency simply because a technology is mentioned.

Where possible, distinguish between:

* strong practical experience
* working knowledge
* exposure
* certification/training only

### Leadership and responsibility

Identify evidence of:

* people management
* team leadership
* project/program leadership
* budget responsibility
* strategic responsibility
* decision-making
* stakeholder management
* client management
* vendor management
* cross-functional leadership
* ownership/accountability

Only classify something as demonstrated when supported by the CV.

### Seniority

Estimate the person's professional seniority based on the actual career history.

Use categories such as:

* entry
* junior
* mid
* senior
* lead
* manager
* senior manager
* director
* executive

If uncertain, indicate the uncertainty rather than inventing precision.

### Strengths

Identify the person's strongest demonstrated professional areas.

These should emerge from the CV.

For example, a person might have:

* very strong project delivery
* strong stakeholder management
* moderate technical knowledge
* limited people management
* deep gambling industry experience

Another person could have a completely different profile.

### Gaps / limitations

Identify areas where the CV provides weak or no evidence that might matter for employment matching.

IMPORTANT:

A lack of evidence is NOT proof that the person lacks the skill.

Use language such as:

* "No clear evidence in CV"
* "Limited evidence"
* "Some exposure"
* "Strong evidence"

Do not state that someone cannot do something unless the CV clearly supports that conclusion.

### Transferable capabilities

Identify capabilities that could reasonably transfer between industries or professional roles.

For example:

* complex project delivery
* stakeholder management
* process optimization
* technical troubleshooting
* financial analysis
* team leadership
* regulatory knowledge

Explain why the capability appears transferable where useful.

---

## 3. Important principle

The AI must distinguish between:

**What the person has actually demonstrated**

and

**What the person might potentially be capable of doing.**

The profile must be evidence-based.

Do not inflate experience.

Do not assume that similar job titles mean identical responsibilities.

Do not treat a mention of a technology as professional expertise.

Do not treat a single project as deep industry experience.

Do not invent missing information.

---

## 4. JSON output

Gemini must return valid JSON using the following general structure:

{
"professional_summary": "",
"seniority": {
"level": "",
"evidence": ""
},

"experience_areas": [
{
"area": "",
"strength": "strong|moderate|limited|incidental",
"years": null,
"recency": "current|recent|older|unknown",
"seniority": "",
"evidence": ""
}
],

"industries": [
{
"industry": "",
"strength": "strong|moderate|limited|incidental",
"years": null,
"recency": "current|recent|older|unknown",
"depth": "deep|moderate|surface|unknown",
"evidence": ""
}
],

"skills": [
{
"skill": "",
"category": "technical|managerial|operational|analytical|commercial|communication|leadership|domain|other",
"strength": "strong|moderate|limited|exposure",
"evidence": ""
}
],

"technologies_tools_methodologies": [
{
"name": "",
"type": "technology|tool|platform|methodology|certification|other",
"proficiency": "strong|working_knowledge|exposure|training_only",
"evidence": ""
}
],

"leadership_and_responsibility": [
{
"area": "",
"strength": "strong|moderate|limited|evidence_only",
"evidence": ""
}
],

"strengths": [
{
"area": "",
"reason": ""
}
],

"gaps_or_limited_evidence": [
{
"area": "",
"assessment": "limited_evidence|no_clear_evidence",
"reason": ""
}
],

"transferable_capabilities": [
{
"capability": "",
"evidence": "",
"potential_applications": []
}
]
}

The JSON structure itself should remain generic and should not contain hardcoded role categories.

For example, do NOT create fields such as:

`project_management_score`

`product_management_score`

`gambling_experience_score`

because these are user-specific and should emerge from the CV.

---

## 5. profile_text generation

After extracting the JSON, generate `profile_text`.

It should be a concise but information-rich natural-language summary suitable for later job matching.

It should describe:

* who the person is professionally
* their strongest experience areas
* industries/domains where they have meaningful experience
* important technical/tools knowledge
* leadership/responsibility
* transferable capabilities
* relevant limitations or weaker areas

Do not write it as a CV or cover letter.

It is an internal professional profile for an employment matching system.

## 6. Gemini API implementation

Use:

`GEMINI_API_KEY`

from environment variables.

Never hard-code the API key.

Create a reusable Gemini service/module appropriate to the existing codebase, for example:

`gemini_service.py`

Implement something similar to:

`generate_user_profile(cv_text, existing_profile_text=None)`

The function should return:

* `profile_text`
* `profile_json`

---

## 7. When Gemini should run

Do NOT call Gemini every time the job scraper runs.

Profile generation should happen only when:

1. A user explicitly requests "Generate/Update Profile", OR
2. The user's CV has been uploaded or changed.

Do not automatically regenerate profiles during normal job scraping.

---

## 8. Existing profile protection

If Gemini fails:

* do not overwrite the existing `profile_text`
* do not overwrite the existing `profile_json`

If Gemini returns invalid JSON:

* attempt safe parsing/repair if appropriate
* otherwise treat the request as failed
* preserve the existing profile

---

## 9. Cost control

The profile generation is a relatively infrequent operation.

Do not send unnecessary repeated requests.

Store the generated profile in the database so it can be reused by the matching engine.

Also store metadata if practical:

* `profile_generated_at`
* `profile_model`
* `profile_version`

If these columns are easy to add, add them to `users`.

Do not create another table.

---

## 10. Security and logging

Never log:

* Gemini API keys
* complete CV contents
* complete profile contents

It is fine to log:

* user ID
* request started
* request succeeded/failed
* model name
* timestamp
